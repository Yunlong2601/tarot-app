## How they name themselves
1. **Keyword-stuffed descriptive names** ("Tarot Card Meanings & Reading", "Daily Tarot Card & Astrology", "Tarot & Numerology"). Built for store search, easy to find, impossible to remember or trademark.
2. **Brand from an existing business or deck** (Labyrinthos, Golden Thread). Works when you already own an audience.
3. **Coined or short brand words** (Nummi, Taroter, Tarotoo, Tarotap). Often "Tarot" plus a twist, which keeps search relevance and is memorable.
4. **Brand + descriptor subtitle** (Taroter: Tarot Cards Scanner). The store shows a name and a subtitle, so the brand carries trust and the subtitle carries the keywords. Check the current character limits for name and subtitle on both stores (Apple's has been 30 characters each; verify).
5. **Education framing** (School, Academy). Positions the app as learning rather than fortune-telling.

## What it means for Veilscan
- **Veilscan fits type 4 well**, for example "Veilscan: Tarot Card Scanner" (28 characters). The brand is short and invented; the subtitle holds the search words. "Scanner" and "scan" are in the name, which is the point of difference.
- **The scan idea is not unique.** At least Taroter, Thoth Tarot School and one MWM app already offer camera scanning. My earlier claim that scanning is "uncommon" (in the App Store checklist and the plan) should read "less common". The reviewer note should still lead with it, but also lead with the AI reflection and the journal.
- **Check before committing:** exact-name and near-name availability in both stores, trademark searches (Singapore IPOS, plus the US and EU if you sell there), a free domain and social handle. "Veilscan" has not been checked.
- Alternatives in the same style if "Veilscan" is taken: a calm invented word + "Tarot Scanner" subtitle. Avoid "Rider-Waite" in the name (trademark) and avoid copying Taroter's pattern too closely.
